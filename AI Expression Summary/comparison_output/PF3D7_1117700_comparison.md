## Qualitative Assessment

### Tone and Style

**Summary A:** Written in a narrative, descriptive style with a flowing structure. Each experiment is presented as a bullet point with a single descriptive sentence summarizing the key finding. The overall summary reads like a well-crafted abstract. The tone is interpretive and confident, sometimes making strong claims (e.g., "unprecedented antisense transcription"). Experiment scores use a compact letter-grade confidence format (e.g., "4A", "3B").

**Summary B:** Written in a structured, data-driven style with explicit quantitative metrics (fold changes, percentiles, p-values) prominently featured. Uses a more systematic and tabular approach with clear section headers. The tone is more measured and empirical, letting the numbers speak. Scoring uses separate Biological Importance and Confidence integers with clear definitions.

**Comparison:** Summary A is more readable in a traditional narrative sense and may be more approachable for a quick overview. Summary B is more systematic and data-rich, providing explicit quantitative context that allows users to independently assess significance. Summary B's approach of separating biological importance from statistical confidence is more transparent and reproducible. However, Summary A's flowing style is easier to digest for a first encounter with the data.

**Preferred tone and style: Summary B** — its systematic, quantitative approach with transparent scoring criteria is more appropriate for a database tool where users need to make their own assessments.

### Technical Detail Level

**Summary A:** Provides TPM values, percentile rankings, and fold-change estimates within experiment descriptions. However, it rarely provides p-values and the confidence scoring system (letter grades A-E) is less transparent. Some experiments are described with rich quantitative detail (e.g., specific TPM values at specific timepoints) while others are more qualitative.

**Summary B:** Consistently provides fold changes, percentile rankings of fold changes, and p-values where available. The scoring system is explicitly defined with clear cutoffs for both biological importance (based on FC percentile) and confidence (based on p-value). This makes every score independently verifiable. Summary B also explicitly flags when differential expression data is not available ("C: N/A").

**Comparison:** Summary B provides substantially more consistent technical detail, particularly regarding statistical support. The explicit inclusion of p-values and fold-change percentiles allows users to critically evaluate each claim. Summary A provides good expression-level detail (absolute TPM values, percentile rankings) but is less consistent with statistical metrics. Summary B's transparent scoring rubric is a significant advantage for a database tool.

**Preferred technical detail level: Summary B**

## Overall Summary

Both summaries are competent and capture the essential biology of this highly expressed blood-stage gene. They share 19 core observations and 4 key insights, with few genuine contradictions. However, they differ substantially in their approach to presenting and scoring data.

**Summary B is the preferred summary for PlasmoDB users** for the following reasons:

1. **Transparent and reproducible scoring**: Summary B's dual scoring system (Biological Importance based on fold-change percentile; Confidence based on p-value) is explicitly defined with clear cutoffs, making every score verifiable and interpretable. Summary A's combined letter-grade system (e.g., "4A") is less transparent — the criteria for assigning confidence letters are vague.

2. **Quantitative rigor**: Summary B consistently reports fold changes, fold-change percentiles, and p-values, enabling users to form their own judgments. Summary A often omits p-values and relies more on qualitative descriptions.

3. **Appropriate flagging of statistical limitations**: Summary B explicitly marks experiments lacking differential expression statistics as "C: N/A" rather than assigning potentially misleading confidence scores. This is critical for a database tool.

4. **More unique observations**: Summary B captures 12 unique observations versus 9 for Summary A, including important findings like ring-stage translational repression, strain-specific expression differences, and antisense dynamics in the sporozoite transition.

5. **More informative headline**: Summary B's headline communicates specific comparative biology rather than general descriptors.

**Where Summary A excels:**
- More readable narrative flow for quick comprehension
- Better highlighting of the dramatic antisense transcription finding (given 5A importance)
- More interpretive insights (8 vs 6 unique), which some users may find helpful
- Includes mRNA stability data (which Summary B lacks access to and should not be penalized for)

**Key concern with Summary B's scoring**: Some scores appear inconsistently calibrated. For example, the Sir2 KO experiment receives BI: 5 despite showing only FC 1.1 (top 4.3%), and the Erythrocytic expression time series also receives BI: 5 despite showing "very low and stable expression with minimal fold changes." According to Summary B's own criteria, a BI of 5 requires FC percentile <10%, and while the Sir2 result (4.3%) qualifies, labeling a 1.1-fold change as "Exceptional" seems misleading. Similarly, the "minimal fold changes" in the erythrocytic time series should not score BI: 5. These inconsistencies partially undermine the transparency advantage.

**Key concern with Summary A's scoring**: The combined importance-confidence scores (e.g., "1A", "2B") make it difficult to separate biological effect size from statistical support. An experiment scored "1A" (low importance, high confidence) conveys a very different meaning than "4C" (high importance, moderate confidence), but the system conflates these dimensions.

### Final Verdict

**Summary B is recommended as the more useful summary for PlasmoDB users**, primarily due to its transparent scoring framework, consistent quantitative reporting, and systematic structure that supports independent evaluation of the data. However, the scoring inconsistencies noted above should be addressed in future iterations of the tool.
