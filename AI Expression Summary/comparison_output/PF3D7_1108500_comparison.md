## Qualitative Assessment

### Tone and Style

**Summary A:** Written in a narrative, descriptive style. The prose is structured around thematic groupings with flowing paragraph-level summaries followed by bulleted experiment details. The tone is qualitative and somewhat interpretive, using phrases like "dramatic cyclical expression," "robust late-stage peak," and "prominent antisense transcription." Scoring uses a letter-based confidence system (e.g., 5A, 4B) that is compact but requires the reader to reference the scoring rubric. TPM values and percentile ranks are emphasized, while fold changes and p-values are largely absent from the experimental descriptions.

**Summary B:** Written in a more structured, data-driven analytical style. The tone is quantitative and precise, consistently reporting fold changes, percentile rankings, and p-values. The summary makes heavy use of structured headers, bolded terms, and a consistent format for each experiment entry (BI score, Confidence score). The opening summary uses bullet points for key findings with specific quantitative values. The style is more like a systematic review or structured database annotation.

**Comparison:** Summary A reads more like a scientific review narrative and is more accessible to readers who prefer flowing text, while Summary B is structured more like a database-quality annotation with rigorous quantitative reporting. Summary B's consistent inclusion of fold changes and p-values provides more immediately actionable information for a scientist evaluating the data. Summary A's narrative style, while readable, sometimes sacrifices precision (e.g., "10-40 fold" without specifying which datasets). Summary B's use of markdown formatting (headers, bold, structured lists) enhances scannability and rapid information retrieval.

**Preferred tone and style: Summary B**

### Technical Detail Level

**Summary A:** Provides TPM values and percentile rankings extensively. Fold changes are occasionally mentioned in approximate terms (e.g., "~32-fold increase," "24-fold higher"). P-values are absent from all experimental descriptions. The scoring system (5A, 4B, etc.) combines importance and confidence into a single alphanumeric code, but the confidence categories (A through E) are less intuitive and less granular than numerical p-value-based categories. Stage-specific details are given as time points in hours with corresponding TPM values.

**Summary B:** Consistently reports fold changes, percentile rankings (as "top X% upregulated/downregulated"), and p-values where available. The dual scoring system (Biological Importance 0–5 and Confidence 0–5) with explicit criteria tied to fold-change percentiles and p-value thresholds is more transparent and reproducible. The technical detail is higher overall — for example, specifying FC −24.6 (top 4.5%, p=6.5e-24) for sporozoite silencing versus Summary A's "24-fold higher expression (73.70 vs 3.00 TPM)." Summary B also explicitly notes when p-values are unavailable ("C: N/A") versus when results lack statistical support ("C: 0").

**Comparison:** Summary B provides substantially more technical detail, particularly in statistical support (p-values), standardized fold changes, and percentile-based importance rankings. Summary A provides more raw expression values (TPM) and percentile ranks but lacks statistical significance measures. For a scientist assessing which results to trust, Summary B's inclusion of p-values and explicit confidence scoring is far more informative. Summary A's scoring system, while functional, is less transparent — the relationship between a letter grade (A–E) and actual statistical evidence is less immediately interpretable than Summary B's p-value thresholds.

**Preferred technical detail level: Summary B**
