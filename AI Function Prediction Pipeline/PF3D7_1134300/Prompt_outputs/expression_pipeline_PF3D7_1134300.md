## Predicted Functions

1. A nuclear-localized protein involved in cell cycle regulation or mitotic chromosome organization during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle, with peak expression during schizogony.
2. A component of the schizont-stage transcriptional or chromatin remodeling machinery required for DNA replication and cell division.
3. A protein functioning in intracellular vesicle trafficking or protein sorting, potentially linking nuclear and endomembrane compartments during schizont development.
4. An essential protein involved in coordinating schizont maturation and merozoite formation, possibly through regulation of invasion gene expression.
5. A structural or regulatory protein contributing to inner membrane complex or apical organelle biogenesis during late-stage parasite development.

## Summary

This gene encodes an essential protein that is most highly expressed during the schizont stage of the <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i> intraerythrocytic developmental cycle (IDC). Transcriptomic data consistently show strong upregulation from trophozoite to schizont stages (up to ~50-fold), with marked downregulation in ring and early trophozoite stages. Quantitative proteomics confirms high ring-stage protein abundance that decreases into trophozoite/schizont stages, suggesting protein is synthesized in schizonts for use in the subsequent ring stage. The strongly negative piggyBac fitness score (−1.44) indicates essentiality. Coexpression with cell cycle regulators, condensin, cohesin, and nuclear proteins strongly supports a role in cell division or nuclear organization.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene shows a clear schizont-stage expression peak across multiple IDC time-course experiments. In the Otto et al. dataset, expression rises from 27.69 TPM (0hr/ring) through a trough at 16hr (5.87 TPM, FC −4.7) and peaks at 40–48hr (22–25 TPM). The Kucharski high-resolution time course shows progressive upregulation from ring (3.39 TPM) peaking at 38hr (33.25 TPM, FC 9.8, top 24.4% upregulated). In the Toenhake dataset, expression at T05 (ring) is 128.34 TPM, drops to 6.18 TPM at T20 (FC −20.8, top 21.4% downregulated), then rises dramatically to 445.63 TPM at T40 (FC 3.5, top 38.1% upregulated). The Wichers 2019 dataset shows significant upregulation from mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite (FC 8.2, p = 1.74e-08, top 5.7% upregulated, effect size 3.34). UTR-Seq confirms peak at 40hr (FC 3.2, top 1.2% upregulated, p ≈ 0, effect size top 2.9%). Schizont-to-trophozoite comparisons consistently show strong downregulation in trophozoites (Josling et al.: FC −15.3, p = 4.38e-09). The gene is strongly downregulated in sporozoites compared to blood stages (Hoffmann: FC −146.3, top 6.7% downregulated, p = 2.37e-16) and in oocysts vs. ring stages. Gametocyte data shows relatively stable, moderate expression. In severe vs. uncomplicated malaria, the gene is upregulated in severe cases (FC 3.7, top 12.2% upregulated, p = 0.004).

**Additional Data (Proteomics, Mutagenesis, PTMs):** The piggyBac mutant fitness score of −1.442 is strongly negative, indicating the gene is essential for asexual blood-stage growth. Mass spectrometry detected the protein in 8 of 63 samples with 46 unique peptides, indicating moderate but reliable protein expression. Quantitative proteomics from the IDC phosphoproteome study shows the protein is most abundant at ring stage (log2 = 4.91) with strong decreases to trophozoite (log2 = 3.46, fold difference −2.7, top 0.7% downregulated) and schizont (log2 = 3.17, fold difference −3.3, top 1.8% downregulated). This inverse pattern—high mRNA at schizont but high protein at ring—is consistent with "just-in-time" translation where schizont-stage transcripts are translated for use in the following ring stage or during invasion. The PfCRK4 experiment shows minimal change upon CRK4 destabilization (FC −1.07, not significant), suggesting this protein is not a direct CRK4 target. One phosphorylation site (S31) was detected, suggesting potential post-translational regulation. 

**Coexpression:** The WGCNA module reveals coexpression with genes enriched for nuclear localization (16/50 genes), along with cytoplasmic (7) and mitochondrial (7) proteins. Key coexpressed genes include: condensin complex subunit 2, cohesin complex subunit RAD21, cyclin-dependent kinase regulatory subunit, cyclin-dependent kinase binding protein, spindle assembly abnormal protein 6, and repressor of RNA polymerase III transcription MAF1. GO terms include regulation of cell cycle, mitotic spindle organization, mitotic chromosome condensation, chromatin binding, and DNA topoisomerase binding. This coexpression profile strongly supports a role in nuclear processes related to cell division—chromosome condensation, cohesion, and mitotic spindle assembly during schizogony. Additional coexpressed genes (inner membrane complex protein, apical polar ring protein APR1, FIKK kinase) suggest coordination with merozoite biogenesis.

**Localisation:** No hyperLOPIT data was available. However, the coexpression GO term enrichment strongly points to nuclear localization (16 of 50 coexpressed genes are nuclear-annotated).

**Binding:** No gold standard interactions or above-threshold MAPX interactions were available, precluding direct inference from protein-protein interaction data.

## Confidence

Confidence in the prediction of a schizont-stage, nuclear, cell-cycle-related function is **moderate to high**. Multiple independent transcriptomic datasets consistently demonstrate a schizont-stage expression peak, and the coexpression module is strikingly enriched for cell division and chromosome organization genes. The essential nature of the gene (piggyBac score −1.44) is consistent with a fundamental role in parasite replication. The inverse mRNA-protein timing pattern (high schizont mRNA, high ring protein) is a well-established feature of invasion-related and cell-cycle genes in <i>P. falciparum</i>. The main limitation is the absence of localization data and protein interaction data, which prevents confirming the nuclear assignment or identifying specific complex membership. The coexpression module contains diverse functional categories (mitochondrial, endomembrane), which somewhat dilutes confidence in a single precise function.

## Experimental Validation

To confirm the predicted function, several approaches could be pursued: (1) Epitope tagging (e.g., HA or GFP) followed by immunofluorescence microscopy across IDC stages to confirm nuclear localization and timing of expression. (2) Conditional knockdown using the glmS ribozyme or DD/TetR system to assess effects on schizogony, nuclear division, and merozoite formation, with phenotypic readout via flow cytometry and microscopy. (3) Co-immunoprecipitation or BioID/TurboID proximity labeling to identify interaction partners, particularly testing for association with condensin, cohesin, or CDK complexes. (4) Bioinformatic analysis using HHpred or AlphaFold structural prediction to identify potential domains or structural homology to known cell-cycle regulators. (5) ChIP-seq or CUT&Tag if the protein is chromatin-associated, to identify genomic binding sites and potential transcriptional regulatory roles.
