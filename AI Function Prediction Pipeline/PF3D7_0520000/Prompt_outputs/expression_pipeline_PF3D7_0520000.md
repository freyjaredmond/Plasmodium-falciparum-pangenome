## Predicted Functions

1. Structural component of the 40S ribosomal small subunit, directly participating in cytoplasmic translation during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle.
2. Essential housekeeping protein required for global protein synthesis throughout asexual blood-stage development.
3. Contributor to ribosome biogenesis and assembly of the small ribosomal subunit in the cytoplasm.
4. Participant in translational regulation during stage transitions of the <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i> life cycle, particularly active during ring and early trophozoite stages.
5. Structural scaffolding protein mediating rRNA binding within the 40S subunit to ensure translational fidelity.

## Summary

This gene encodes a 40S ribosomal protein subunit in <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i>. All five orthogonal data types converge on this conclusion: the gene is highly expressed during early asexual blood stages (rings/trophozoites) and downregulated in late schizonts and sporozoites; it co-expresses with dozens of ribosomal proteins; it localises to the ribosome compartment; its protein physically interacts with the full complement of 40S and 60S ribosomal subunit proteins; and it is essential for parasite survival based on a strongly negative piggyBac fitness score (−3.0). The protein functions as a core structural constituent of the ribosome required for mRNA translation.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is among the most highly expressed during asexual blood stages, consistently ranking in the top 1–4% by expression (e.g., TPM ~1,444–2,798 at ring stage, expression rank 39–55). It shows clear cell-cycle periodicity: upregulated during rings and early trophozoites (peaking at ~16 hpi with 2–3 fold increases across multiple studies including Otto, Toenhake, Chappell DAFT-Seq, UTR-Seq, and Subudhi), then declining 5–14 fold during late schizonts and merozoites (e.g., 0hr vs 48hr FC = −14.3 in Subudhi; 24hr vs 48hr FC = −12.7). This temporal profile is characteristic of translation machinery genes needed for rapid growth in early stages. Expression drops dramatically in sporozoites (FC = −5.9 vs blood stage, Gomez-Diaz; FC = −37.6 vs blood stage, Hoffmann), consistent with reduced translational demand. The polysomal data (Bunnik) shows a striking 14.5-fold decrease in polysome-associated mRNA vs steady-state at ring stage, suggesting tight translational regulation. The antisense data from DAFT-Seq shows the antisense transcript for 3D7 vs IT comparison has a top 1.1% upregulated fold change, and the IDC antisense time course shows peaks at 8hr and 16hr (FC 2.4–2.5, top 2–5%), suggesting potential antisense-mediated regulation. Microarray data from Bozdech/Llinas across 3D7, DD2, and HB3 consistently shows downregulation at 40–48 hpi (top 1.5–3.6% of downregulated genes). Gametocyte stages show moderate downregulation. The gene is essential based on the piggyBac mutant fitness score of −2.999.

**Additional data (Proteomics/Mutagenesis/PTMs):** The protein is abundantly detected by mass spectrometry (26/63 samples positive, 175 summed unique peptide counts, 21 unique peptides across samples), confirming robust expression. The piggyBac insertion mutagenesis fitness score of −2.999 indicates the gene is essential for blood-stage growth, consistent with a core ribosomal component. One acetylation site was detected, which is common for ribosomal proteins. Quantitative proteomics shows modest decreases from ring to schizont (−1.6 fold, top 32% downregulated) and ring to trophozoite (−1.3 fold), consistent with transcriptomic trends. The PfCRK4 proteome shows a 1.78-fold upregulation at 37 hpi when CRK4 is destabilised (top 5.4% upregulated), and a −1.49-fold decrease from 29 to 37 hpi under normal conditions, further supporting dynamic regulation during the IDC.

**Coexpression:** The top 50 coexpressed genes are overwhelmingly ribosomal proteins and translation factors: ~30 are 40S/60S ribosomal protein subunits, alongside elongation factors (EF-1α, EF-1γ, EF-2), translation initiation factor eIF2α, and aminoacyl-tRNA ligases (lysine-tRNA ligase, threonine-tRNA ligase). GO term enrichment is dominated by "structural constituent of ribosome" (24 genes), "translation" (25 genes), "cytosolic small ribosomal subunit" (15 genes), "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (13 genes), and "RNA binding" (30 genes). This is an exceptionally coherent coexpression module pointing to a core translational function.

**Localisation:** HyperLOPIT assigns this protein to the "ribosomes" compartment. The GO terms for this niche are dominated by ribosomal functions: "structural constituent of ribosome" (49 genes), "translation" (46 genes), "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (35), "cytosolic small ribosomal subunit" (20). Importantly, 23 of the top 50 coexpressed genes co-localise in this same ribosomal niche, providing strong cross-validation between the coexpression and localisation datasets.

**Binding:** The gold standard interaction dataset lists 72 interactors comprising essentially the complete set of 40S small subunit proteins (~30 proteins including S2–S30) and 60S large subunit proteins (~40 proteins including L2–L44, plus ubiquitin-L40 fusion). This is the expected interactome for a core ribosomal subunit protein. Gold standard GO terms are dominated by "structural constituent of ribosome" (63), "translation" (59), "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (41), and "cytosolic small ribosomal subunit" (30). MAPX data confirms these interactions with high recurrence (most 40S/60S proteins recovered at 5–7 of 7 time points), demonstrating stable association throughout the IDC. A few non-ribosomal MAPX hits at lower recurrence (1 count) likely represent noise or transient associations.

## Confidence

Confidence is very high. All five data types independently and consistently identify this gene as encoding a core 40S ribosomal small subunit protein. The coexpression module is among the most coherent possible (almost exclusively ribosomal proteins). The localisation assignment to the ribosome compartment directly confirms this. The binding data shows physical interactions with the complete ribosome. The transcriptomic profile — high expression in early asexual stages, strong downregulation in late stages and sporozoites, and cell-cycle periodicity — matches the well-characterised behaviour of ribosomal protein genes in <i>P. falciparum</i>. The strongly negative piggyBac fitness score confirms essentiality. There are no contradictions across data types. The only minor uncertainty is the exact identity of which 40S subunit protein this is (e.g., S9, S13, S14, or another not explicitly named in the data), but the functional role is unambiguous.

## Experimental Validation

The ribosomal function could be confirmed by: (1) tagging the endogenous gene with GFP or HA and performing immunofluorescence to confirm cytoplasmic ribosomal localisation; (2) co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry to verify association with the 40S subunit and 80S ribosome; (3) sucrose gradient polysome profiling to demonstrate co-sedimentation with 40S, 80S, and polysome fractions; (4) conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or DD/Shield system) to demonstrate reduced global translation (measured by puromycin incorporation) and growth arrest; (5) bioinformatic confirmation through sequence alignment and structural modelling against known eukaryotic 40S ribosomal protein structures, and cross-referencing with the cryo-EM structure of the <i>P. falciparum</i> ribosome.
