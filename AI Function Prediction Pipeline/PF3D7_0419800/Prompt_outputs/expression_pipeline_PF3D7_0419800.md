## Predicted Functions

1. Ribosome biogenesis factor involved in rRNA processing and ribosomal subunit assembly in the nucleolus
2. Component of the small subunit (SSU) processome required for pre-rRNA maturation
3. Nucleolar protein involved in RNA modification or methylation during ribosome assembly
4. RNA-binding protein participating in nucleolar RNA metabolism and ribosome export
5. Essential housekeeping factor contributing to translational capacity during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle

## Summary

This gene most likely encodes a nucleolar ribosome biogenesis factor in <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i>. Its transcriptional profile peaks at early ring/early blood stages and declines markedly through trophozoite and schizont stages, consistent with active ribosome production in preparation for rapid growth. Coexpression analysis strongly links it to nucleolar proteins, U3 snoRNA-associated subunits, rRNA-processing helicases, and ribosomal assembly factors. Its essential nature (piggyBac fitness score −3.3) and detection in mass spectrometry across blood stages support a critical role in ribosome biogenesis.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is highly expressed at ring stage (e.g., 248 TPM at 0 hr in IDC constant-temperature study, expression rank ~651, top 12%) and shows strong, consistent downregulation as the IDC progresses toward schizont stages. In Otto et al., expression drops from 51 TPM at 0 hr to 4.75 TPM at 48 hr (FC −10.8, top 4% downregulated). In Toenhake et al. antisense data, it starts at 165 TPM (rank 22, top 0.4%) and drops 44-fold by T25. In Kucharski et al., expression at 46 hr is 5-fold lower than at 4 hr (top 4.1% downregulated). DAFT-seq data corroborate significant downregulation at 24 hr (FC −10.3 antisense, p = 8 × 10⁻¹⁸, top 4% significant). This early-peaking, rapidly declining profile is characteristic of ribosome biogenesis genes that are required during the growth-preparatory ring phase. The gene is downregulated in sporozoites relative to blood stages and oocysts, consistent with reduced proliferative demands.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are overwhelmingly nucleolar ribosome biogenesis factors. The most frequent GO term is "nucleolus" (19/50 genes), followed by "nucleus" (15), "small-subunit processome" (5), "rRNA processing" (4), "RNA helicase activity" (4), and "ribosomal large subunit assembly" (4). Specific coexpressed genes include U3 snoRNA-associated proteins (UTP12, UTP21, IMP2), multiple ATP-dependent RNA helicases (DDX31, MTR4, DBP10, DDX47, DRS1, MAK5, DBP8), rRNA methyltransferases, ribosome assembly proteins (RRB1, RPF1), and RNA polymerase III subunits. This module composition is a hallmark signature of the ribosome biogenesis pathway.

**Additional Data (Proteomics, Mutagenesis, PTMs):** The protein is detected in 12 of 63 mass spectrometry samples with 11 unique peptides (41 total), confirming expression at the protein level during blood stages. Quantitative proteomics shows modest upregulation from ring to trophozoite (fold difference 1.4, top 19.1% upregulated) and ring to schizont (1.3), indicating relatively stable protein abundance despite declining mRNA—typical for ribosome biogenesis factors whose proteins persist after transcriptional shutdown. The piggyBac mutagenesis fitness score of −3.3 indicates the gene is essential for blood-stage growth, consistent with a core role in ribosome production. Three phosphorylation sites suggest regulatory modification, common for nucleolar proteins.

**Binding (MAPX):** MAPX interactors include multiple ribosomal proteins (S24, S5, S15A, S23, L34, L32, L36), the nucleolar TRM6 tRNA methyltransferase subunit (also in the coexpression module), protein BCP1 (a ribosome biogenesis factor), and several RNA-binding proteins. The interactor GO terms include "nucleus" (26), "RNA binding" (8), "ribosome biogenesis" (1), and ribosomal subunit terms. Physical association with ribosomal proteins and RNA-processing factors strongly supports a role in ribosome assembly.

## Confidence

Confidence is **high**. The transcriptomic, coexpression, proteomic, mutagenesis, and interaction data all converge on a nucleolar ribosome biogenesis function. The coexpression module is remarkably coherent—19/50 genes are annotated as nucleolar, and the module includes canonical ribosome biogenesis machinery (U3 snoRNP components, rRNA helicases, ribosome assembly factors). The transcriptional profile (early-peaking, rapidly declining) is the expected signature for ribosome biogenesis genes. The essential phenotype (fitness score −3.3) is consistent. The only limitation is the absence of hyperLOPIT localization data and gold-standard protein complex assignments, which prevents direct confirmation of nucleolar localization and specific complex membership.

## Experimental Validation

Nucleolar localization could be confirmed by endogenous tagging with GFP or HA followed by immunofluorescence microscopy with co-staining for the nucleolar marker PfNOP1. Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or DD/Shield system) followed by metabolic labeling of nascent rRNA (e.g., ³H-uridine or EU incorporation) would test whether depletion impairs rRNA processing. Northern blotting or primer extension of rRNA intermediates upon knockdown would identify specific processing steps affected. Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry would define the protein complex. Bioinformatically, domain analysis (searching for known ribosome biogenesis domains such as WD40, brix, or sigma-70-like) and homology searches against yeast ribosome biogenesis factors could identify the specific ortholog and predict its precise mechanistic role.
