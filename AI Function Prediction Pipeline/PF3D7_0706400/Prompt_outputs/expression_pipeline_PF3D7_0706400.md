## Predicted Functions

1. Structural component of the cytoplasmic ribosome, likely a 60S ribosomal protein subunit involved in translation during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle.
2. Essential protein required for mRNA translation during asexual blood-stage growth.
3. Contributor to ribosome biogenesis and assembly in the parasite cytoplasm.
4. Protein involved in translational regulation during the trophozoite stage of the IDC, where peak expression is observed.
5. Potential role in translational control during sexual stage development, given moderate expression changes during gametocytogenesis.

## Summary

This gene encodes a ribosomal protein that is an essential structural constituent of the <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i> cytoplasmic ribosome. All five orthogonal data types — transcriptomics, proteomics, coexpression, localisation, and protein interaction — converge on a ribosomal/translation function. The gene is highly expressed during asexual blood stages, peaking in trophozoites (~16–24 hpi), is localised to the ribosome compartment, co-expresses almost exclusively with other ribosomal proteins, and physically interacts with dozens of 40S and 60S ribosomal subunit proteins. Its strongly negative piggyBac mutant fitness score (−2.84) indicates it is essential for parasite survival.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is moderately to highly expressed during asexual blood stages (TPM ~200–1200 across experiments), with peak expression in the trophozoite window (~16–24 hpi). In the Otto et al. time course, expression peaks at 24 hr (TPM 500, rank 217, top 4%) and declines at 40–48 hr. The Wichers et al. IDC data shows significant downregulation from mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite (FC −1.6, p = 1.9e-11, effect size top 6.3% downregulated) and from late trophozoite to early schizont (FC −2.4, p = 1.9e-5, top 1.8% downregulated). The Toenhake IDC sense data confirms upregulation from ring (T05) to trophozoite stages (T20: FC 5.4, T25: FC 5.7). Expression is dramatically reduced in sporozoites versus rings (FC −19.6 antisense, Zanghi) and in schizonts/late stages. The antisense strand shows very low expression at rings/0 hr but peaks at ~16 hr (Subudhi: FC 44.8 from 0 hr, top 2.4%), suggesting antisense regulation during the trophozoite stage. In gametocytes, expression decreases from stage I through stage V (Young et al. array: FC −1.9 by day 6, top 8.3% downregulated). The gene is preferentially expressed in male versus female gametocytes (Lasonder: 8.1-fold higher in males, antisense). Overall, the transcriptomic profile — high trophozoite expression, decline at schizonts, low in sporozoites — is characteristic of a translation machinery gene active during the period of maximal protein synthesis.

**Additional data (Proteomics, PTMs, Mutagenesis):** The protein is detected in 9 of 63 mass spectrometry samples with 4 unique peptides and 16 total unique peptide counts, consistent with moderate abundance. Quantitative proteomics shows the protein is upregulated 1.8-fold from ring to trophozoite (top 5.3% of upregulated proteins), then downregulated 1.6-fold from trophozoite to schizont (top 11.9% downregulated), mirroring the transcriptomic trophozoite peak. The protein has 2 phosphorylation sites, consistent with regulated ribosomal protein function. The piggyBac mutant fitness score is −2.84, indicating the gene is essential for blood-stage growth. In the apicoplast vs. ER proteomics, both reference and comparison values are 0, meaning the protein is not associated with either compartment, consistent with cytoplasmic ribosome localisation.

**Coexpression:** The top 50 WGCNA co-expressed genes are overwhelmingly ribosomal proteins (approximately 38 of 50), including both 40S and 60S subunit components, elongation factors (EF-1α, EF-2), tRNA ligases, and translation initiation factor eIF2α. The dominant GO terms are "structural constituent of ribosome" (26 genes), "translation" (27), "RNA binding" (26), "cytosolic small ribosomal subunit" (16), and "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (15). This near-exclusive co-expression with translation machinery genes strongly supports a ribosomal function.

**Localisation:** HyperLOPIT assigns this protein to the "ribosomes" subcellular compartment. The GO terms for this compartment are dominated by "structural constituent of ribosome" (49), "translation" (46), and "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (35). Twenty-three of the 50 WGCNA co-expressed genes are also found in the same ribosome compartment, providing independent validation. This direct localisation evidence is among the strongest indicators of function.

**Binding/Interactions:** Gold standard interactions include 71 ribosomal proteins spanning both the 40S and 60S subunits, including multiple proteins that also appear in the coexpression data. The gold standard GO terms are overwhelmingly ribosomal: "structural constituent of ribosome" (64), "translation" (59), "cytosolic large ribosomal subunit" (40), "cytosolic small ribosomal subunit" (31). MAPX interactions recapitulate these findings with high-frequency interactions (6–7 recurrences) with L13-2, L7-3, L21, L15, S3A, S19, and many other ribosomal proteins. The breadth of interactions across both subunits indicates the protein is an integral structural component of the mature ribosome.

## Confidence

Confidence in the ribosomal protein prediction is very high. All five data types converge unambiguously: ribosome localisation (hyperLOPIT), exclusive coexpression with ribosomal/translation genes, physical interactions with >70 ribosomal proteins, trophozoite-peaking expression (when translation is maximal), trophozoite-peaking protein abundance, and essential mutant fitness. There are no contradictions across data types. The only minor uncertainty is whether the protein belongs to the 40S or 60S subunit; the interaction with proteins from both subunits and the gold standard including both subunit members is expected for any ribosomal protein, as they interact within the assembled ribosome. The antisense expression pattern is notable but does not contradict the core functional prediction.

## Experimental Validation

The ribosomal protein function could be confirmed by: (1) epitope tagging (e.g., HA or GFP fusion) followed by immunofluorescence to confirm ribosomal co-localisation with known ribosomal markers; (2) polysome profiling followed by western blot to confirm the protein co-sediments with polysomal fractions; (3) conditional knockdown (e.g., glmS or DD-FKBP system) to assess effects on global translation rates (puromycin incorporation assay) and parasite growth; (4) co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry to confirm physical association with ribosomal subunit components; and (5) BLASTp or HMM-based homology searches against eukaryotic ribosomal protein databases (e.g., RPG database) to assign a specific ribosomal protein family membership if not already annotated.
