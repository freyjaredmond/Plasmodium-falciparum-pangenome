## Predicted Functions
1. Ribosome biogenesis factor involved in rRNA processing and ribosomal subunit assembly in the nucleus
2. RNA methyltransferase or RNA modification enzyme contributing to tRNA/rRNA maturation
3. Translation-associated nuclear factor coordinating ribosome export or pre-ribosome maturation
4. Nucleolar protein involved in pre-rRNA processing and small/large subunit processome function
5. General RNA-binding protein with roles in mRNA splicing or RNA quality control in the nucleus

## Summary
This gene most likely encodes a nuclear/nucleolar protein involved in ribosome biogenesis, specifically rRNA processing and ribosomal subunit assembly. Its coexpression network is dominated by ribosomal proteins, translation initiation factors, tRNA ligases, and RNA modification enzymes. The gene localises to the nucleus (hyperLOPIT "nucleus1" compartment), is expressed primarily during early asexual blood stages (rings and early trophozoites) with declining expression in later stages, is essential for blood-stage growth (piggyBac fitness score = 0.0), and shows enriched polysomal association. Together, these data strongly support a role in ribosome biogenesis.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene shows moderate-to-high expression during ring and early trophozoite stages (e.g., ~81–236 TPM at 0 hr in multiple IDC datasets), with consistent downregulation toward late trophozoite, schizont, and late blood stages (fold changes of −2 to −8 at 40–48 hr timepoints across Otto, Subudhi, Kucharski, and Toenhake datasets). Antisense transcription follows a similar declining pattern. Strong downregulation in sporozoites relative to blood stages (e.g., FC = −6.9, Hoffmann; FC = −24.2, Zanghi antisense) and in salivary gland sporozoites versus oocysts suggests blood-stage–biased function. The gene is highly male-enriched in gametocytes (Male TPM = 633.57 vs Female 144.29, FC = −4.4 in Male→Female comparison, Lasonder), and moderately expressed in stage II gametocytes (276 TPM) declining in stage V (90.6 TPM). In the polysomal versus steady-state comparison (Bunnik), the gene shows dramatic polysomal enrichment at trophozoite (FC = 10.8, top 2.1%) and schizont (FC = 7.9, top 3.7%) stages with a significant differential expression effect size (top 8.1% downregulated in steady-state vs polysomal, p = 0.00028), indicating its mRNA is actively translated. The early-ring peak expression profile is consistent with ribosome biogenesis genes, which are typically expressed before the peak of translation.

**Additional Data:** The piggyBac mutant fitness score of 0.0 indicates the gene is essential for asexual blood-stage growth. Only 1 of 63 mass spectrometry samples detected the protein (2 unique peptides), suggesting low protein abundance or transient expression, consistent with a nuclear/nucleolar regulatory factor rather than an abundant structural protein. Two phosphorylation sites were identified, which is common for nuclear regulatory proteins. In the PfCRK4 proteomics dataset, the protein shows mild downregulation at 37 hpi when CRK4 is destabilized (FC = −1.41, top 10.3% of downregulated genes), suggesting potential regulation during the trophozoite-to-schizont transition, possibly linked to DNA replication–coupled ribosome biogenesis.

**Coexpression:** The top 50 WGCNA coexpressed genes are overwhelmingly enriched for ribosome biogenesis and translation machinery: multiple 40S and 60S ribosomal proteins (S6, S7, S19, L7, L13-2, L15), translation initiation factors (eIF3B, eIF3M, eIF4A-III, eIF6, SUI1), tRNA ligases (glycine, alanine, threonine), tRNA modification enzymes (pseudouridine synthase, methylthiolation, threonylcarbamoyl transferase, TRM6), rRNA methyltransferases, RNA helicases (PRP43, DBP8, RuvB-like 3), and nucleolar GTP-binding protein 1. The dominant GO terms are nucleus (30/50), RNA binding (10), structural constituent of ribosome (7), translation (7), nucleolus (5), with specific terms like ribosomal large subunit biogenesis, rRNA processing, maturation of LSU-rRNA, and preribosome large subunit precursor. This profile is a hallmark of ribosome biogenesis.

**Localisation:** HyperLOPIT assigns this protein to the "nucleus1" compartment. The GO terms for this niche include nucleus (214), RNA binding (63), nucleolus (22), rRNA processing (12), preribosome large subunit precursor (9), small-subunit processome (9), and translation initiation factor activity (6). Six coexpressed genes co-localise to this same nuclear compartment, including RNA helicases (PRP43, eIF4A-III), RNA methyltransferase (RNA cytosine C5-methyltransferase), and an Alba domain protein — all consistent with nucleolar ribosome biogenesis. The overlap between coexpression and colocalisation strongly reinforces a nuclear/nucleolar ribosome assembly role.

**Binding:** No gold standard or high-confidence MAPX protein interactions were available.

## Confidence
Confidence in this prediction is **high**. The convergence across four independent data types — coexpression with ribosome biogenesis factors, nuclear/nucleolar localisation, early blood-stage expression peak characteristic of ribosome biogenesis genes, and essentiality — provides robust support. The polysomal enrichment data further confirms that this gene's product is actively translated during the IDC. The only uncertainty is the precise molecular function (e.g., rRNA methyltransferase vs. assembly factor vs. RNA helicase cofactor), which cannot be distinguished without binding data or domain annotation. The low mass spectrometry detection (1/63 samples) is somewhat unusual for an essential gene but is consistent with low-abundance nuclear regulatory factors. No contradictions were observed between data types.

## Experimental Validation
To confirm the ribosome biogenesis function, conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS or DD/TMP system) should be performed followed by assessment of rRNA processing intermediates via northern blot or primer extension. Polysome profiling after knockdown would reveal defects in ribosomal subunit production. Immunofluorescence with an epitope-tagged version should confirm nucleolar localisation, particularly during ring/early trophozoite stages. Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry (co-IP/MS) would identify interaction partners and potentially place the protein within a known pre-ribosomal complex. Bioinformatically, hidden Markov model searches (HHpred, Phyre2) against the protein sequence could identify conserved ribosome biogenesis domains (e.g., methyltransferase, DEAD-box helicase, or WD40 repeats common in processome components) that would narrow the specific molecular function.
