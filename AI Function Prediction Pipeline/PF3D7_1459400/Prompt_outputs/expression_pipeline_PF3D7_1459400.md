## Predicted Functions

1. Endoplasmic reticulum-resident protein involved in protein folding, quality control, or trafficking of exported proteins to the Maurer's cleft and host cell
2. Component of the secretory/export pathway facilitating transport of PEXEL/HT-containing proteins through the ER to the parasitophorous vacuole and beyond
3. Chaperone or co-chaperone participating in the unfolded protein response or ER stress management during the ring-to-early trophozoite transition
4. Regulatory protein coordinating the expression or sorting of virulence-associated exported proteins (PHISTs, HYPs, RIFINs)
5. ER-associated membrane protein contributing to lipid metabolism or membrane biogenesis required for Maurer's cleft formation

## Summary

This gene encodes an essential, ER-localized protein whose expression peaks during ring and early blood stages and declines sharply during trophozoite development. Its coexpression network is dominated by Maurer's cleft and host-cell exported proteins (PHIST, HYP families, RESA, EMP1-trafficking protein), while its subcellular localization is firmly within the ER. Together with its strongly negative piggyBac fitness score (−3.14), ubiquitin-binding post-translational modification, and high steady-state mRNA but reduced polysomal association, the data suggest it functions as an essential ER-resident factor in the protein export and quality control pathway that delivers parasite effectors to the host erythrocyte.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is highly expressed during ring stages (expression rank top 4–11% at 0 hr/early ring in multiple studies) and consistently downregulated during mid-trophozoite stages (e.g., −5.9-fold late ring to mid-trophozoite in Wichers et al., top 4.3% downregulated; −3.7-fold at 22 hpi in Kucharski; −8.3-fold at 16 hr in Subudhi IDC). Expression recovers somewhat during schizogony. This ring-stage peak followed by trophozoite decline is a hallmark of genes involved in early export and Maurer's cleft biogenesis. The gene is markedly downregulated in sporozoites versus blood stages (−28.7 to −48.5-fold in Hoffmann), confirming blood-stage specificity. Antisense transcription shows modest, largely non-significant changes. Notably, the UTR-seq study (Chappell) shows the gene among the most significantly downregulated at 48 hr versus 0 hr (top 1.2% downregulated, p-value effectively 0), reinforcing tight cell-cycle regulation.

**Additional Data (Proteomics, Mutagenesis, PTMs):** The protein is detected in 21 of 63 mass spectrometry samples with 34 unique peptides, confirming it is an abundantly expressed protein. The piggyBac mutant fitness score of −3.14 indicates the gene is essential for asexual blood-stage growth. Quantitative proteomics shows modest upregulation from ring to schizont (1.5-fold, top 19.9% upregulated), suggesting protein accumulates even as mRNA declines, consistent with early translation and stable protein persistence. The steady-state versus polysomal comparison shows significant enrichment in the steady-state fraction (p = 0.0007, top 9.9% upregulated effect size), meaning the mRNA is relatively under-translated—potentially indicating post-transcriptional regulation. The presence of ubiquitin-binding sites (2 sites) suggests involvement in ubiquitin-dependent quality control or protein sorting. ER localization by hyperLOPIT (ER1 compartment) anchors the protein firmly in the secretory pathway.

**Coexpression:** The top 50 coexpressed genes are overwhelmingly dominated by exported proteins: 18/50 annotated to Maurer's cleft, 14 to host cell, including multiple PHIST family members (PHISTa, PHISTb, PHISTc), HYP proteins (hyp2, hyp6, hyp12, hyp13, hyp15), RESA (×2), EMP1-trafficking protein, KIC1, KIC4, and the food vacuole transporter. Additional coexpressed genes include Sec24A (COPII vesicle coat, ER exit), kelch protein K13 (associated with vesicular trafficking and artemisinin resistance), PX1 (phosphoinositide-binding), and Golgi protein 1—all consistent with a role in protein export and vesicle-mediated transport from the ER.

**Localisation:** HyperLOPIT assigns the protein to the ER1 compartment. The GO terms enriched in this niche include ER membrane, protein targeting to ER, COPII-coated vesicle transport, protein folding in ER, SNARE binding, vesicle-mediated transport, Maurer's cleft, and intracellular protein transport. One coexpressed gene ("conserved Plasmodium protein, unknown function") co-localizes in the same ER1 niche, reinforcing the export pathway connection.

**Binding:** No gold standard or above-threshold MapX interactions are available, so direct physical interactors cannot inform the prediction.

## Confidence

Confidence in the ER-localized, protein-export-related function is **high**. Three independent data types converge: (1) ER1 subcellular localization from hyperLOPIT, (2) strong coexpression with Maurer's cleft/exported proteins and secretory pathway components, and (3) a ring-stage expression peak consistent with the timing of export machinery establishment. The essential fitness score further supports a core cellular role. The main uncertainty is the precise molecular function—whether it acts as a chaperone, sorting receptor, or structural ER component—since no binding partners or enzymatic domains are explicitly identified. The absence of interaction data is the primary limitation. The ubiquitin-binding sites could suggest quality control, but this remains speculative without functional validation.

## Experimental Validation

Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or DD/FKBP system) timed to ring-stage should reveal defects in protein export to the Maurer's cleft, which can be assessed by immunofluorescence of known exported proteins (e.g., KAHRP, PfEMP1, RESA). Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry from ring-stage parasites would identify physical interaction partners and confirm export pathway involvement. Live-cell imaging with a GFP-tagged version would validate ER residency and potential co-localization with ER exit sites or transitional ER markers. BioID/TurboID proximity labeling would capture transient interactions with cargo proteins. Finally, transmission electron microscopy after conditional depletion could reveal ultrastructural defects in Maurer's cleft formation or ER morphology.
