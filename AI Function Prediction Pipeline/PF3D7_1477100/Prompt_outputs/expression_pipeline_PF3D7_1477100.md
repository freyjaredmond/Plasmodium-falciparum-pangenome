## Predicted Functions

1. Trophozoite-stage metabolic enzyme involved in pyrimidine or folate biosynthesis within the mitochondrion
2. Mitochondrial matrix protein contributing to nucleotide metabolism during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle
3. Oxidoreductase or redox-associated enzyme functioning in the trophozoite stage with roles in metabolic homeostasis
4. Enzyme involved in amino acid or energy metabolism (e.g., related to pyruvate kinase, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase pathway) peaking in mid-IDC
5. Mitochondrial-targeted protein with a role in drug/xenobiotic response or resistance

## Summary

This gene most likely encodes a mitochondrial metabolic enzyme active primarily during the trophozoite stage of the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle. Its expression peaks at 16–24 hours post-invasion, with strong transcriptional upregulation (up to 30-fold) at these time points, followed by sharp decline in schizonts. Coexpression with pyrimidine biosynthesis genes (dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, aspartate carbamoyltransferase, orotate phosphoribosyltransferase), mitochondrial import machinery, and redox proteins strongly supports a mitochondrial metabolic function. The highly negative piggyBac fitness score (−2.67) indicates the gene is essential for asexual blood-stage growth.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is very lowly expressed at ring stage (TPM ~0–4, expression percentile >86–96%, meaning bottom ~5–14% of expressed genes) but shows dramatic upregulation peaking at 16–24 hpi. In the Kucharski high-resolution time course, fold changes from 4hr to 16hr and 22hr reach 24.6× and 30.1× (top 1–1.6% of upregulated genes). In the UTR-Seq dataset, the 0hr-to-16hr comparison shows a 23.6-fold increase (top 0.1% upregulated, p = 3.2×10⁻²⁵), and 0hr-to-24hr shows 11.1-fold (top 0.6%, p = 9.5×10⁻¹²). The Wichers IDC dataset confirms strong upregulation from early ring to late ring (26.3-fold, p = 2.4×10⁻⁵, top 0.8% upregulated, effect size top 2.1%), followed by significant downregulation from mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite (−10.5-fold, top 1.8% downregulated, p = 3.3×10⁻⁸). The Toenhake dataset shows peak expression at T20–T25 (47–59 TPM, top 2.5–2.8% upregulated from T05), declining sharply by T40 (−14.4-fold from T25, top 2.2% downregulated). Real-time transcription/decay data (labelled fractions) show 12–15-fold increases at 12–20 hpi (top 0.3–0.5%), confirming active transcription during the trophozoite stage. The Subudhi constant-darkness IDC similarly shows a 48-fold increase at 16hr (top 10.2%). Expression in mosquito stages and gametocytes is negligible. The schizont-to-trophozoite comparison (Josling PfBDP1HA) shows 4.4-fold upregulation in trophozoites (top 15.1%, p = 8.4×10⁻⁵, effect size top 2.1%). Collectively, the transcriptomic profile defines a trophozoite-peaking gene with sharp induction and subsequent decline.

**Additional Data (Proteomics, Mutagenesis, PTMs):** The piggyBac mutant fitness score is −2.671, indicating the gene is essential for blood-stage survival. Quantitative proteomics (Ring vs Trophozoite) shows a slight increase (fold difference 1.2, top 37.9% upregulated), while Trophozoite to Schizont shows a decrease (fold difference −1.4, top 26.0% downregulated), consistent with trophozoite-stage peak protein abundance. The protein is detected in proteomics (reference values ~4.0 log2), confirming it is expressed at the protein level during the IDC.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are strongly enriched for mitochondrial functions: mitochondrion (17 genes), TIM23 complex components (TIM9, TIM10, TIM17, TIM50), mitochondrial import, and mitochondrial matrix proteins. Key metabolic coexpressed genes include dihydroorotate dehydrogenase, aspartate carbamoyltransferase, and orotate phosphoribosyltransferase — three sequential enzymes in the *de novo* pyrimidine biosynthesis pathway — as well as hydroxymethyldihydropterin pyrophosphokinase-dihydropteroate synthase (folate pathway). Redox partners (thioredoxin-like protein, plasmoredoxin, 10 kDa chaperonin) and energy metabolism enzymes (pyruvate kinase 2, phosphoenolpyruvate carboxykinase, glutamate dehydrogenase) are also coexpressed. GO terms highlight mitochondrion (17), nucleus (20), *de novo* pyrimidine nucleobase biosynthetic process (2), response to xenobiotic stimulus (7), and protein import into mitochondrial matrix (3). This coexpression signature strongly suggests the gene product operates in the mitochondrion during trophozoite-stage metabolism, potentially in or closely associated with pyrimidine biosynthesis.

**Localisation:** No hyperLOPIT localisation data is available for this gene. However, the coexpression network overwhelmingly points to mitochondrial localisation.

**Binding:** No gold standard or MAPX interaction data is available.

## Confidence

Confidence in the prediction of a mitochondrial, trophozoite-stage metabolic function is **high**. The transcriptomic data is internally consistent across >10 independent experiments, all showing peak expression at 16–24 hpi with rapid decline. The coexpression network provides a coherent functional context — mitochondrial pyrimidine biosynthesis and redox metabolism — rather than a heterogeneous mix of themes. The essential phenotype (piggyBac score −2.67) is consistent with a core metabolic role. The main uncertainty is the precise enzymatic function: while the coexpression strongly suggests involvement in pyrimidine or related nucleotide/cofactor biosynthesis, without direct functional annotations, the gene could serve an accessory or regulatory role in this pathway rather than being a catalytic enzyme. The absence of localisation and binding data prevents independent confirmation of the mitochondrial assignment.

## Experimental Validation

To confirm the predicted function: (1) GFP or mCherry tagging of the endogenous locus could verify mitochondrial localisation via co-staining with MitoTracker. (2) Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or TetR-DOZI system) during the trophozoite stage, followed by metabolomics profiling, would reveal whether pyrimidine intermediates (dihydroorotate, orotate, UMP) or related metabolites accumulate or are depleted. (3) Co-immunoprecipitation with tagged dihydroorotate dehydrogenase or other coexpressed mitochondrial enzymes could test for physical interactions. (4) Bioinformatically, remote homology detection using HHpred or AlphaFold structure prediction compared against known enzyme structures could identify the catalytic domain and assign a specific enzymatic function. (5) Heterologous expression and enzymatic activity assays with candidate substrates (pyrimidine intermediates, folate precursors) would definitively assign biochemical function.
