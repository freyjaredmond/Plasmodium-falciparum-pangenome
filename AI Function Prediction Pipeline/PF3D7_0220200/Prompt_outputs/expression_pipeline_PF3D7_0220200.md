## Predicted Functions
1. Trophozoite-stage tRNA modification or processing enzyme involved in translational regulation during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle (IDC)
2. Nuclear RNA-modifying enzyme contributing to tRNA maturation and ribosome biogenesis
3. Essential housekeeping gene involved in protein translation machinery, peaking in expression during mid-IDC (trophozoite stage)
4. Component of a tRNA/rRNA processing complex required for efficient translation during active parasite growth
5. Enzyme involved in nucleotide or RNA methylation with a role in gene expression regulation during asexual blood stages

## Summary
This gene encodes an essential protein that is highly expressed during the trophozoite stage of the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle, with peak expression around 16–24 hours post-invasion. Its expression is strongly downregulated in late schizonts, gametocytes, and mosquito stages. Coexpression analysis reveals association with tRNA-modifying enzymes, ribosomal proteins, translation initiation factors, and chaperonins, pointing to a role in translational regulation or tRNA processing. The highly negative piggyBac fitness score (−3.604) confirms essentiality. Minimal detection by mass spectrometry suggests the protein may be low-abundance or transient.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is moderately-to-highly expressed during asexual blood stages (expression rank ~top 4–10% at ring/trophozoite). Across multiple IDC time courses (Otto, Kucharski, Toenhake, Wichers, Subudhi, Siegel, Chappell), expression peaks at 16–24 hpi (trophozoite), with fold changes of 2.5–4.5× relative to 0 hr, then declines sharply by 32–48 hpi (schizonts/late stages). For example, in Otto et al., 0hr→24hr shows FC=2.5 (top 24.3% upregulated), and in Kucharski, 4hr→16hr shows FC=4.3 (top 10.1%). Toenhake antisense data shows progressive downregulation from T05 to T35 (FC=−26.7, top 3.5% downregulated), indicating strong antisense regulation. The gene is dramatically downregulated in sporozoites (Hoffmann: FC=−238.4, top 3.8% downregulated) and oocysts relative to blood stages. In gametocyte data (Young et al.), expression decreases from day 1 to later stages (FC=−1.7 to −1.8). Sexually committed schizonts show downregulation versus asexual schizonts (FC=−1.8, top 2.8% downregulated, Pelle). Steady-state vs polysomal comparisons (Bunnik) show reduced polysomal association at ring stage (FC=−12.3, top 6.5%), suggesting translational regulation. The Wichers IDC data shows significant upregulation from early ring to late ring (FC=2.8, p=0.002, top 25.7% effect size) and significant downregulation from mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite (FC=−1.8, p=1.5×10⁻⁵). The UTR-Seq data (Chappell) shows universally significant p-values (p=0) across all comparisons, with progressive downregulation from 0hr to 32hr (FC=−3.1).

**Additional Data (Proteomics/Mutagenesis):** The piggyBac mutant fitness score of −3.604 indicates the gene is essential for blood-stage growth. Mass spectrometry detection is very low (1 out of 63 samples, 1 unique peptide), suggesting the protein is either low-abundance, rapidly turned over, or difficult to detect by standard proteomics. Quantitative proteomics (PfCRK4 study) shows significant downregulation from 29 to 37 hpi (FC=−2.5, p=0.00116, top 4.9% downregulated), consistent with peak expression at trophozoite stage declining toward schizonts. The IDC proteome data shows modest upregulation from ring to trophozoite (FC=1.4, top 19.1%).

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are enriched for tRNA modification enzymes (tRNA dimethylallyltransferase, tRNA pseudouridine synthase, tRNA 4-demethylwyosine synthase, TRM6), aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases (valine-tRNA ligase, glycine-tRNA ligase, threonine-tRNA ligase), ribosomal proteins (40S S7, S6, S19; 60S L6, L7, L7-3), translation initiation factors (eIF3B, eIF3D, eIF6), chaperonins (T-complex proteins alpha/delta/theta), and RNA processing factors (RNA methyltransferase, exoribonuclease, RNA polymerase III subunit). Top GO terms include nucleus (27), cytoplasm (12), cytosol (10), RNA binding (7), structural constituent of ribosome (6), translation (5), protein folding (5), and tRNA modification (2). This strongly supports a function in tRNA processing or modification linked to the translational machinery.

**Binding Data:** No gold standard interactions or above-threshold MapX interactions were available.

**Localisation:** No hyperLOPIT localisation data was available.

## Confidence
Confidence is moderate-to-high. The transcriptomic profile is robust and consistent across >10 independent studies: peak expression at trophozoite stage, strong downregulation in non-blood stages, and essentiality. The coexpression network strongly and coherently points toward tRNA modification/translation, with minimal ambiguity across themes. The main limitation is the absence of localisation data, interaction data, and substantial proteomic evidence (only 1 peptide detected). The antisense transcript data shows an interesting inverse pattern (upregulated at late stages when sense is down), which may indicate regulatory complexity. The low proteomic detection despite high mRNA abundance and essentiality is somewhat puzzling but could reflect rapid protein turnover or poor peptide recovery.

## Experimental Validation
To confirm the predicted tRNA modification/processing function: (1) Generate a conditional knockdown (e.g., glmS or DD-FKBP system) and perform tRNA profiling (e.g., tRNA-seq or northern blots) to detect modification defects; (2) Immunoprecipitate the tagged protein and identify RNA substrates by CLIP-seq or RIP-seq, expecting enrichment for tRNAs; (3) Perform co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry to confirm interactions with the coexpressed tRNA-modifying enzymes and translation factors; (4) Use GFP-tagging to determine subcellular localisation (predicted nuclear/nucleolar based on coexpression GO terms); (5) Bioinformatically, search for conserved RNA-modifying enzyme domains (e.g., methyltransferase, pseudouridine synthase) via HHpred or InterPro, and perform phylogenetic analysis against known tRNA modification enzymes across Apicomplexa.
