## Predicted Functions

1. Nuclear protein involved in transcriptional regulation or chromatin-associated processes during the late asexual intraerythrocytic developmental cycle (schizont/merozoite stages).
2. Component of a nuclear signaling complex linking cyclic nucleotide or kinase signaling to gene expression regulation.
3. Protein involved in pre-erythrocytic to erythrocytic stage developmental transitions, with a role in coordinating invasion-related gene expression.
4. Nuclear factor contributing to cell cycle progression during schizogony and merozoite formation.
5. Regulatory protein involved in RNA processing or spliceosomal function within the nucleus.

## Summary

This gene encodes a nuclear protein that is essential for blood-stage parasite survival, as evidenced by a strongly negative piggyBac mutagenesis fitness score (−2.81). It is moderately expressed throughout the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle (IDC), with peak expression during late trophozoite and schizont stages (40–48 hpi), and its protein abundance decreases from ring to trophozoite/schizont stages. The gene localizes to the nucleus, and its coexpression network includes kinases, invasion-related proteins, and signaling molecules. Its antisense transcript shows notable upregulation during late IDC stages. Together, the data suggest a nuclear regulatory role during schizogony, potentially linking signaling pathways to transcriptional control of invasion and egress genes.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene shows moderate baseline expression (~30–160 TPM across studies) with consistent upregulation during the late IDC (40–48 hpi). In the Otto et al. time course, expression rises from 39.5 TPM at 0 hr to 137.4 TPM at 48 hr (3.5-fold, top 7% upregulated). The Subudhi et al. IDC data shows a dramatic increase to 602 TPM at 40 hr (20.9-fold) and 511 TPM at 48 hr (17.7-fold). Kucharski and Tripathi data confirm peak expression around 38–42 hpi (6.0-fold and 5.4-fold vs 4 hr). Antisense transcription is notably upregulated during mid-to-late IDC stages, particularly in Toenhake et al. data (T20: 8.7-fold, top 2.5%; T25: 6.3-fold, top 4.2%) and DAFT-Seq data (16 hr antisense: 3.2-fold, top 2.3%; 48 hr: 2.5-fold, top 0.4%). The gene is downregulated in sporozoites relative to blood stages (Hoffmann: −7.6-fold in cultured sporozoites) and in gametocytes relative to asexual stages (Young et al. array: −1.6 to −1.8 fold across gametocyte stages). The Wichers 2019 IDC data confirms progressive upregulation from late trophozoite to early schizont (2.0-fold, top 28.1%, p = 0.001). In the sexually committed schizont comparison, the gene is upregulated 1.5-fold in sexual vs asexual schizonts (top 18.3%). The gene is downregulated in the in vitro sporozoite comparison (−9.8-fold, top 19.1%) and at the oocyst stage relative to rings.

**Additional Data (Proteomics, Mutagenesis, PTMs):** The piggyBac mutagenesis fitness score of −2.81 indicates the gene is essential for blood-stage growth. Mass spectrometry data is sparse (only 1 of 63 samples detected, 1 unique peptide), suggesting the protein may be low abundance or difficult to detect. Quantitative proteomics show the protein is more abundant at ring stage (log2 = 4.58) than trophozoite (3.81) or schizont (3.58), with fold differences of −1.7 (ring to trophozoite, top 11.5% downregulated) and −2.0 (ring to schizont, top 15.6% downregulated). This inverse relationship between rising mRNA and declining protein during the IDC may reflect translational regulation or rapid protein turnover. The PfCRK4 proteomics data shows the protein increases 1.64-fold from 29 to 37 hpi (top 33.5%, p = 0.004), consistent with late IDC function. One phosphorylation site was detected, indicating post-translational regulation.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes include several invasion-related proteins (rhoptry neck protein 12, glideosome-associated protein 40, glideosome associated protein with multiple membrane spans 3, rhomboid protease ROM4, cytoadherence linked asexual protein 9, serine repeat antigen 1), signaling molecules (cGMP-dependent protein kinase, 3',5'-cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterase beta, serine/threonine protein kinases), inner membrane complex components (inner membrane complex suture component, filament assembling protein), and several unknown function proteins. GO term enrichment highlights nucleus (12 genes), cell surface (7), cytoplasm (7), endoplasmic reticulum (6), entry into host (5), inner membrane pellicle complex (5), and protein binding (5). The strong representation of invasion/egress machinery and signaling kinases among coexpressed genes strongly supports a role in regulating schizont-stage processes.

**Localisation (HyperLOPIT):** The protein localizes to the "nucleus1" compartment. The GO terms enriched in this compartment are dominated by RNA binding, mRNA splicing/spliceosome components, chromatin binding, transcription regulation, and cell cycle regulation. One coexpressed gene (TH1 domain-containing protein, putative) is also found in this nuclear compartment. TH1 domain proteins are often involved in chromatin regulation or nuclear organization, supporting a chromatin/transcription-related function.

**Binding (Protein Interactions):** No gold standard interactions are available. MAPX interactions include repressor of RNA polymerase III transcription MAF1 (recurring in 2 conditions), golgin subfamily A member 6-like protein, lsm12 (RNA binding), trafficking protein particle complex subunit 13, diphthamide biosynthesis protein 2, dual specificity protein phosphatase, and conserved unknown proteins. The interactor GO terms highlight nucleus (3), cytoplasm (3), negative regulation of transcription by RNA polymerase III (2), RNA polymerase III core binding (2), phosphatase activity, and protein phosphorylation. The interaction with MAF1, a known regulator of RNA polymerase III, and lsm12 (an RNA-binding protein involved in mRNA processing) supports a nuclear role in transcriptional regulation or RNA metabolism.

## Confidence

**Moderate-to-high confidence** in the prediction of a nuclear, essential, late-IDC regulatory protein. The nuclear localization is robustly supported by HyperLOPIT data, and essentiality is strongly indicated by the piggyBac score of −2.81. The late-IDC expression peak is consistently observed across multiple independent RNA-seq and microarray studies. The coexpression with invasion/egress genes and signaling kinases is coherent with a schizont-stage regulatory function. One notable concern is the discrepancy between rising mRNA (late IDC) and declining protein (ring > trophozoite > schizont in TMT proteomics), which could indicate translational delay, rapid turnover, or technical limitations of proteomics detection (only 1 peptide detected in mass spec). The MAPX interactions with MAF1 and lsm12 provide some mechanistic clues but are based on co-fractionation and require validation. The coexpression module contains diverse themes (invasion, signaling, membrane, nuclear), which is typical for schizont-stage genes but reduces specificity of functional inference.

## Experimental Validation

Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the auxin-inducible degron or glmS ribozyme system) during the late IDC would confirm essentiality and timing of function. ChIP-seq or CUT&RUN could test whether the protein associates with chromatin, particularly at promoters of invasion-related genes. Immunofluorescence microscopy with an epitope-tagged version would confirm nuclear localization and track dynamics across the IDC. Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry would validate the predicted interactions with MAF1 and lsm12. RNA-seq following conditional knockdown at the schizont stage would reveal transcriptional targets and test whether invasion/egress genes are downregulated, consistent with the coexpression data. Bioinformatically, domain analysis and structural modeling (e.g., via AlphaFold) could identify potential DNA/RNA-binding or protein-interaction domains that would further clarify mechanism.
