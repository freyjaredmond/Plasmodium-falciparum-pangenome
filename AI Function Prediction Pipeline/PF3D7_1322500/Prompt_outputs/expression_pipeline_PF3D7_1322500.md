## Predicted Functions

1. Schizont/merozoite-stage protein involved in erythrocyte invasion or egress, potentially functioning at the inner membrane pellicle complex or cell surface.
2. Glideosome- or motor complex-associated protein contributing to parasite motility during invasion and sporozoite gliding.
3. Endoplasmic reticulum-resident protein involved in protein trafficking or secretory pathway function required for invasion ligand maturation.
4. Structural component of the basal complex or pellicle contributing to cell morphogenesis during schizogony.
5. Signaling-associated protein involved in cyclic nucleotide or calcium-mediated signal transduction during stage transitions.

## Summary

This gene encodes an essential protein that peaks in expression during late blood stages (schizonts, 32–44 hpi) and is also highly expressed in sporozoites. Its coexpression network is enriched for invasion-related, glideosome, pellicle, and cell-surface proteins. Localization to the ER is consistent with processing through the secretory pathway. The strongly negative piggyBac fitness score (−2.787) confirms essentiality during blood-stage growth. Together, the data support a role in erythrocyte invasion or egress machinery, likely as part of the secretory/surface protein repertoire deployed during schizont rupture and merozoite release.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene shows a dramatic expression pattern during the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle (IDC). It is moderately expressed at ring stage and strongly upregulated at late trophozoite/schizont stages (32–48 hpi). In Otto et al., fold change from 0hr to 48hr was 3.0× (top 11.3% upregulated). In Kucharski et al., expression rose from 8.78 TPM at 4 hpi to 438.71 TPM at 38 hpi (50-fold, top 3.9%). In Toenhake et al., expression peaked at T40 (345.8 TPM vs 148.2 at T05). Expression dropped sharply at ring/early trophozoite stages — in DAFT-Seq, the 0hr-to-16hr comparison showed −7.5× fold change (top 14.8% downregulated), and 0hr-to-24hr showed −4.9× (top 22.3%). In Wichers et al., early ring to late ring showed −4.3× (top 12.4% downregulated, p = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴), while mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite showed 8.6× upregulation (top 5.3%, p = 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰). Josling et al. confirmed schizont-to-trophozoite downregulation of −36× (p = 1.7 × 10⁻³⁰, top 0.4% significant). The gene is also notably upregulated in sporozoites: in Gomez-Diaz, asexual blood stage to salivary gland sporozoite showed 4.3× (top 4.8%, p = 8.9 × 10⁻⁹); in Zanghi et al., ring to sporozoite showed 7.4-fold effect size (top 5.5%, p = 8.5 × 10⁻²²). Antisense data showed consistent upregulation in sporozoites (83.5× ring-to-sporozoite antisense, top 2.8%). In gametocyte data, expression was relatively stable with modest changes. This late-schizont/sporozoite peak expression profile is characteristic of invasion and motility genes.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are strongly enriched for invasion and motility machinery: glideosome-associated proteins (GAP40, GAPM2, GAPM3), high molecular weight rhoptry protein 2 (RHOPH2), 6-cysteine proteins P36 and P38, MSP7-like protein, serine repeat antigen 1 (SERA1), and cyclase-associated protein. GO terms reflect this: "entry into host" (6 genes), "inner membrane pellicle complex" (6), "cell surface" (6), "apical part of cell" (5), "exit from host cell" (4), "plasma membrane" (4), "microneme" (2), "rhoptry" (2). Signaling components (guanylyl cyclase alpha, cGMP/cAMP phosphodiesterase) and cytoskeletal regulators (ARP1, CLASP, filament assembling protein) are also present, consistent with a merozoite invasion or egress function.

**Localization (hyperLOPIT):** The protein was assigned to the "ER1" compartment. This is consistent with processing through the secretory pathway, as many invasion ligands and surface proteins transit the ER before reaching apical organelles or the parasite surface. The ER1 niche GO terms include "endoplasmic reticulum" (33), "membrane" (23), "protein targeting to ER" (6), "vesicle-mediated transport" (6), "cell surface" (7), and "inner membrane pellicle complex" (3). Only one coexpressed gene — basal complex transmembrane protein 1 — was co-localized in this niche, suggesting the protein may be transiently ER-resident during biosynthesis before trafficking to its functional location.

**Proteomics and additional data:** The protein was detected in only 2 of 63 mass spectrometry samples with 2 unique peptides, indicating low abundance or stage-restricted expression — consistent with schizont-specific expression. In the PfCRK4 destabilization experiment at 37 hpi (schizont stage), the protein showed significant upregulation (fold change 1.29, p = 0.0075, top 32.7% of significant genes), consistent with late-stage expression. The piggyBac mutant fitness score of −2.787 is strongly negative, indicating the gene is essential for blood-stage parasite survival, which aligns with a critical role in invasion or egress.

**Binding:** No gold standard or above-threshold MapX interactions were available for this gene.

## Confidence

Confidence in the prediction of a late-schizont/invasion-related function is high. Multiple independent lines of evidence converge: (1) the sharp transcriptional peak at 32–48 hpi across at least six IDC time-course studies, (2) coexpression with well-characterized invasion (RHOPH2, P36, P38, SERA1) and motility (GAP40, GAPM2/3) genes, (3) strong sporozoite upregulation (consistent with shared invasion/motility machinery between merozoites and sporozoites), and (4) the essential piggyBac fitness score. The ER1 localization is consistent but slightly unusual for a mature invasion protein, potentially reflecting ER-stage capture during hyperLOPIT or a role in protein processing/trafficking. The absence of interaction data limits confirmation of specific complex membership. The low mass spectrometry detection is a minor concern but is explained by stage-restricted expression and the limited schizont representation in many proteomic datasets.

## Experimental Validation

Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or auxin-inducible degron system) timed to late schizont stages could confirm whether loss of this protein impairs egress or invasion, as measured by reduced ring-stage parasitemia in the subsequent cycle. Immunofluorescence assay (IFA) with epitope-tagged parasites should be performed across the IDC to determine whether the protein localizes to the ER transiently before trafficking to the inner membrane pellicle, rhoptries, micronemes, or parasite surface at schizont stage. Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry (Co-IP/MS) at the schizont stage would identify binding partners and confirm association with glideosome or invasion complexes. Invasion assays using cytochalasin D treatment or receptor-blocking antibodies in the knockdown background could distinguish between roles in motility versus receptor-ligand interaction. Bioinformatically, structural prediction (AlphaFold) and domain analysis could identify signal peptides, transmembrane domains, or conserved functional motifs that further refine the functional assignment.
