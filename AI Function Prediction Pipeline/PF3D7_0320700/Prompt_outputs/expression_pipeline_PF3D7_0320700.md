## Predicted Functions

1. Endoplasmic reticulum-resident protein involved in protein folding, quality control, or ER-to-Golgi vesicle transport during the trophozoite-schizont stages of the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle.
2. Component of the ER/endomembrane secretory pathway contributing to protein trafficking and maturation required for merozoite assembly and erythrocyte invasion.
3. Chaperone or accessory factor supporting organellar (ER/apicoplast/mitochondrial) protein biogenesis with roles in DNA replication-coupled processes during schizogony.
4. Membrane-associated protein with cation transport or homeostasis functions in the endoplasmic reticulum during late blood-stage development.
5. Regulator of post-translational processing or GPI-anchor biosynthesis in the ER, contributing to surface protein maturation.

## Summary

This gene encodes an endoplasmic reticulum-localized protein that is most highly expressed during mid-to-late trophozoite and schizont stages of the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle. It is essential for asexual blood-stage growth (piggyBac fitness score = 0.0) and is present across multiple life stages at the protein level. Its coexpression partners include DNA replication licensing factors, organellar ribosomal proteins, and metabolic enzymes associated with apicoplast and mitochondrial biogenesis, consistent with a role in ER-based protein processing, folding, or trafficking that supports organelle biogenesis and schizont maturation during schizogony.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene shows a clear trophozoite–schizont expression peak during the IDC. In the Otto et al. RNA-seq time course, expression rises from 206 TPM at 0 hr to 700 TPM at 32 hr (3.4-fold, top 18% upregulated) and remains elevated at 40–48 hr. The high-resolution Kucharski dataset confirms peak expression at 28–30 hpi (542–584 TPM, 24–26-fold over ring, top 1.6–2.2% upregulated). In Toenhake et al. data, expression drops sharply at T15–T20 (−27-fold from T05), consistent with low ring-stage expression and a sharp mid-cycle peak. The Wichers et al. dataset shows significant upregulation from late ring to mid-trophozoite (7.0-fold, p = 6.7×10⁻⁵, top 31% significant). The gene is markedly downregulated in sporozoites versus blood stages (Gomez-Diaz: antisense FC top 4.5% down; Zanghi: oocyst vs ring −90.6-fold, top 4.2% down), and also reduced in mosquito sporozoites versus in vitro cultures (Eappen: −90.6-fold sense). Gametocyte data shows moderate expression with a female-biased antisense signal (10.2-fold female over male antisense, top 2.2%). Microarray data (Bozdech/Llinas) confirms upregulation in mid-late IDC across 3D7, DD2, and HB3 strains. The severe vs. uncomplicated malaria comparison (Tonkin-Hill) shows a modest 2.7-fold upregulation in severe malaria (top 21% up), though not significant. The PfBDP1 knockdown array shows modest upregulation when PfBDP1 is depleted (1.2-fold, top 11.4%), suggesting partial bromodomain-dependent regulation.

**Proteomics:** The protein is detected in 13 of 63 mass spectrometry samples with 14 unique peptides (49 total peptide counts), indicating reliable detection. Quantitative proteomics shows a 1.5-fold increase from trophozoite to schizont (top 14.9% upregulated), consistent with the transcriptomic peak. The ring-to-schizont fold difference is 1.3 (top 35% up), and the ring-to-trophozoite comparison shows minimal change (−1.1-fold). PfCRK4-regulated proteome data shows a 1.48-fold increase when PfCRK4 is destabilized at 37 hpi (p = 0.028, top 12.6% upregulated), suggesting the protein may accumulate when DNA replication-associated kinase activity is perturbed, consistent with a role coupled to schizogony.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are enriched for apicoplast (16 genes), mitochondrial (13 genes), and nuclear (10 genes) proteins. Key coexpressed partners include three DNA replication licensing factors (MCM2, MCM4, MCM7), DNA topoisomerase 3, apicoplast ribosomal proteins (L21, S9, S10, L9), iron-sulfur cluster assembly protein SufC, acyl carrier protein, ATP synthase subunits, and metabolic enzymes (2-C-methyl-D-erythritol 4-phosphate cytidylyltransferase). GO terms highlight DNA replication (DNA unwinding, pre-replicative complex assembly, MCM complex) and organellar biogenesis (plastid organization, structural constituent of ribosome). This coexpression module reflects schizogony-associated processes: DNA replication, organelle duplication, and biosynthetic functions. The presence of GPI transamidase subunit PIG-U and phosphoenolpyruvate/phosphate translocator further links to ER/secretory pathway and apicoplast import functions.

**Localisation (hyperLOPIT):** The protein localizes to the ER1 compartment. The ER1 niche is enriched for endoplasmic reticulum (33 genes), membrane (23), nucleus (22), ER membrane (18), RNA binding (11), and protein binding (10). Key GO terms include protein targeting to ER, protein folding in ER, GPI anchor biosynthesis, EMC complex, signal peptide processing, COPII vesicle transport, and SNARE-mediated vesicle fusion. Only one coexpressed gene (cation diffusion facilitator family protein) co-localizes to ER1, suggesting the gene's ER function is distinct from the organellar biogenesis theme of its coexpression module, possibly reflecting its role in ER-based processing of proteins destined for organelles or the cell surface.

**Mutagenesis:** The piggyBac mutant fitness score is 0.0, indicating the gene is essential for asexual blood-stage growth. This is consistent with a critical function during the IDC, such as protein folding, trafficking, or organelle biogenesis in the ER.

**Binding:** No gold standard or above-threshold MapX interactions are available.

## Confidence

Confidence in the ER localization and trophozoite-schizont expression timing is high, supported by convergent hyperLOPIT, RNA-seq, and proteomic data. The essentiality call (piggyBac score 0.0) is robust. The specific molecular function remains uncertain: the coexpression module strongly suggests coupling to DNA replication and organellar biogenesis during schizogony, while the ER1 localization points to protein processing/trafficking. These are not contradictory—ER-resident proteins are essential for processing invasion ligands and organellar proteins during schizont maturation. The absence of binding data and the heterogeneity of coexpression GO terms (spanning apicoplast, mitochondrion, and nucleus) reduce precision in functional assignment. The gene's product description is unknown, so the prediction relies entirely on indirect evidence.

## Experimental Validation

Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or TetR-DOZI system) timed to the trophozoite stage could confirm essentiality and reveal phenotypic consequences (invasion defects, organelle biogenesis failure). Immunofluorescence microscopy with ER markers (e.g., BiP/PfERC) would confirm ER localization. Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry could identify direct binding partners and clarify whether the protein participates in ER folding machinery, GPI-anchor synthesis, or vesicle trafficking. BioID/TurboID proximity labeling in the ER context would complement this. Bioinformatically, structural prediction (AlphaFold) and domain analysis (HHpred) could reveal conserved functional domains. Transmission electron microscopy of conditional knockdown parasites could assess organellar morphology defects.
