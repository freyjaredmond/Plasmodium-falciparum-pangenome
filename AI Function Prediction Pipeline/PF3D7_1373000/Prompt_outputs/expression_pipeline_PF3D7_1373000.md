## Predicted Functions

1. Variant surface antigen or exported protein involved in antigenic variation and immune evasion at the infected erythrocyte surface
2. Maurer's cleft-associated transmembrane protein contributing to protein trafficking and host cell remodeling
3. RIFIN/STEVOR family member or functionally related protein mediating cytoadherence and rosetting
4. Exported protein involved in modulation of host erythrocyte aggregation and sequestration
5. Membrane-associated protein contributing to host cell surface display and parasite virulence

## Summary

This gene most likely encodes a variant surface antigen or an exported protein that localizes to Maurer's clefts and/or the infected erythrocyte membrane. Its transcriptional profile—very low and largely undetectable expression during most asexual blood stages with modest upregulation in trophozoites—is consistent with clonally variant gene families. Its strong coexpression with RIFINs, STEVORs, and Pfmc-2TM Maurer's cleft proteins, combined with a highly negative piggyBac fitness score (−2.712), suggests an essential role in host cell remodeling, antigenic variation, or cytoadherence.

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** The gene is expressed at extremely low levels (often 0 TPM, expression rank consistently >90th–98th percentile, meaning bottom 2–10% of expressed genes) across virtually all asexual blood stage time courses (Otto, Kucharski, Toenhake, Wichers, Subudhi, Chappell, Siegel datasets). It shows a modest increase at 16 hpi in some datasets (e.g., Subudhi 0→17.84 TPM at 16 hr, FC 32.6, top 15% upregulated; Kucharski TP1→TP7 FC 5.3, top 7.7%). This early-to-mid trophozoite peak is characteristic of variant surface antigen and exported protein gene families. In the PfBDP1HA dataset, there is a significant upregulation in trophozoites versus schizonts (FC 7.5, top 5.8%; p = 1.6×10⁻⁷, effect size top 0.5%), consistent with genes regulated by bromodomain protein 1, which controls invasion and exported gene programs. In the heat shock mutant study, the gene is significantly downregulated in ΔDHC mutants (effect size top 0.8% downregulated, p = 0.014), suggesting sensitivity to chromatin or trafficking disruptions. Sporozoite and mosquito stage expression is negligible. In gametocyte arrays (Young et al.), the gene shows slight downregulation from day 1 to day 2 (FC −1.6, top 9.2% downregulated), consistent with asexual-stage-biased variant genes. The Le Roch life cycle arrays show early ring-to-late ring downregulation (FC −1.7, top 3.3% downregulated) and late ring-to-early trophozoite upregulation (FC 1.6, top 9.9%), further supporting a trophozoite-peaking expression pattern. The severe versus uncomplicated malaria comparison (Tonkin-Hill) shows a large negative effect size for sense transcript (top 3.5% downregulated), consistent with clonally variant gene expression differences between clinical isolates. Sequestration phenotype data (Kamaliddin) shows the gene is downregulated (FC −2.5) in ICAM1-binding parasites compared to HBEC-5i binding (top 3.0% downregulated), linking it to cytoadherence phenotype variation.

**Coexpression (WGCNA):** The top 50 coexpressed genes are overwhelmingly composed of RIFINs (~33 genes), STEVORs (~6 genes), and Pfmc-2TM Maurer's cleft proteins (~7 genes), plus PHISTa pseudogenes. The dominant GO terms are Maurer's cleft (35 genes), host cell plasma membrane (32), antigenic variation (32), modulation of host erythrocyte aggregation (28), symbiont-containing vacuole (23), and cytoadherence to microvasculature (23). This coexpression signature is essentially diagnostic of the variant surface antigen/exported protein network involved in host cell remodeling and immune evasion.

**Additional Data (Mutagenesis):** The piggyBac insertion mutagenesis fitness score is −2.712, indicating the gene is essential or near-essential for blood-stage growth. This is consistent with genes whose products are critical for erythrocyte remodeling, nutrient acquisition, or immune evasion during the intraerythrocytic cycle. No mass spectrometry peptide data or proteomics fold-change data were provided, preventing proteomic confirmation.

**Localisation:** No hyperLOPIT localisation data was available for this gene. However, the coexpression GO terms strongly implicate Maurer's clefts and host cell plasma membrane as the likely subcellular localization.

**Binding:** No gold standard or MapX interaction data was available, precluding direct identification of binding partners.

## Confidence

Confidence is **moderate-to-high** that this gene encodes a variant surface antigen or Maurer's cleft-associated exported protein. The coexpression data is remarkably coherent, with nearly all top 50 coexpressed genes belonging to RIFIN, STEVOR, or Pfmc-2TM families. The transcriptomic profile—very low baseline expression with trophozoite-biased upregulation and sensitivity to bromodomain protein perturbation—is fully consistent with this assignment. The highly negative piggyBac fitness score supports essentiality. The main limitation is the absence of proteomic, localisation, and interaction data, which prevents independent confirmation of subcellular targeting or complex membership. The consistently low expression across most conditions could also reflect clonal silencing typical of variant gene families, which may cause underestimation of its importance from bulk transcriptomic analyses alone.

## Experimental Validation

To confirm function, the protein should be tagged with GFP or HA for immunofluorescence localization to Maurer's clefts or the erythrocyte surface. Conditional knockdown (e.g., using the glmS ribozyme or DD/Shield1 system) would confirm the essentiality suggested by the piggyBac score and reveal phenotypic consequences (growth, cytoadherence, rosetting). Co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry would identify interaction partners among RIFINs, STEVORs, or Pfmc-2TM proteins. Surface trypsinization of intact infected erythrocytes would test for surface exposure. Bioinformatically, BLAST or HMM searches against RIFIN/STEVOR/Pfmc-2TM domain profiles, signal peptide prediction (SignalP), PEXEL motif identification, and transmembrane domain prediction (TMHMM) would clarify whether this protein belongs to a known exported protein family or represents a novel member of the variant surface antigen network.
