## Predicted Functions

1. Merozoite surface or apical organelle protein involved in erythrocyte invasion during the schizont/merozoite stage of the intraerythrocytic developmental cycle.
2. Rhoptry- or microneme-associated protein contributing to host cell entry and parasite egress.
3. Structural component of the apical complex involved in cytoadherence or host-cell recognition.
4. Signaling or regulatory protein active during late-stage asexual development (schizogony) that coordinates invasion gene expression.
5. Essential protein functioning in merozoite maturation or organelle biogenesis during the transition from schizont to merozoite.

## Summary

This gene encodes a protein that peaks in expression during the late schizont stage of the <i>Plasmodium falciparum</i> intraerythrocytic developmental cycle and is strongly associated with erythrocyte invasion. Transcriptomic data consistently show upregulation in schizonts relative to rings and trophozoites, with dramatic fold changes. Coexpression analysis links it to rhoptry proteins, merozoite surface proteins, and invasion-related genes. The highly negative piggyBac mutant fitness score (−2.609) indicates it is essential for blood-stage growth. Together, these data strongly support a role as an invasion-related protein, likely localized to apical organelles (rhoptries or micronemes).

## Evidence

**Transcriptomics:** Across multiple RNA-seq and microarray time-course studies, the gene shows a clear late-stage expression pattern. In the Otto et al. blood-stage time course, expression drops from 35.6 TPM at 0 hr to 3.75 TPM at 16 hr (FC −9.5, top 5.7% downregulated) but rises to 89–91 TPM at 40–48 hr (FC ~2.5, top ~17% upregulated). DAFT-seq data show significant downregulation at 8 hr (FC −4.7, p = 2.4e-6, top 9.9% significant) and 16 hr (FC −13.5, p = 5.0e-12, top 10.1%) relative to 0 hr, with recovery by 40–48 hr. In the Wichers et al. IDC, the gene is significantly upregulated from mid-trophozoite to late trophozoite (FC 6.6, p = 1.4e-13, top 17.2% significant) and from late trophozoite to early schizont (FC 3.4, p = 1.7e-3). Conversely, it is significantly downregulated from early ring to late ring (FC −5.8, p = 3.0e-4, top 7.5% downregulated effect size). The Toenhake et al. data show massive downregulation from T05 to T25 (FC −271.9, top 3.2% downregulated) with high expression at T05 (186 TPM). The Josling et al. schizont vs. trophozoite comparison shows FC −83.7 (top 4.4% downregulated, p = 9.0e-10, effect size top 0.7%), confirming schizont-peak expression with rapid decline post-invasion. In gametocyte stages, expression is present but lower, and it is downregulated in sporozoites relative to blood stages (Hoffmann et al., FC −54.3 for mosquito sporozoites). The UTR-seq data show highly significant downregulation at 16 hr and 24 hr (p = 0, top 0.0%), with upregulation at 40 hr (FC 2.6, p = 0, top 0.0%).

**Additional Data (Proteomics/Mutagenesis/PTMs):** Only 3 of 63 mass spectrometry samples detected this protein (9 unique peptides, 12 total), consistent with stage-specific expression. The piggyBac mutant fitness score of −2.609 indicates strong essentiality during blood-stage growth, supporting a critical function such as invasion. The protein has a phosphorylation site at position 11, suggesting potential regulatory modification. Low proteomic detection despite moderate transcript abundance may reflect a tightly regulated, stage-specific protein with a short half-life, consistent with invasion machinery.

**Coexpression:** The top 50 coexpressed genes are heavily enriched for invasion-related and apical organelle proteins: rhoptry neck proteins 2, 5, and 11; rhoptry-associated leucine zipper-like protein 1; rhoptry-associated membrane antigen; apical sushi protein; apical asparagine-rich protein AARP; merozoite surface protein 10; Duffy binding-like merozoite surface protein; plasmepsin IX; cytoadherence-linked asexual proteins (CLAG3.1, CLAG3.2); and multiple armadillo-repeat merozoite proteins. GO terms confirm this: "entry into host" (11 genes), "apical part of cell" (8), "rhoptry" (8), "rhoptry neck" (6), "cell surface" (6), "host cell surface binding" (4), and "host cell plasma membrane" (3). This is a remarkably coherent invasion-related coexpression module.

**Binding Data:** No gold standard or MapX interaction data were available.

**Localisation Data:** No hyperLOPIT localisation data were available, which is consistent with either low abundance or with assignment to a compartment not well captured (e.g., rhoptries or micronemes, which have limited representation in hyperLOPIT datasets).

## Confidence

Confidence in the prediction of an invasion-related, schizont-stage function is high. The transcriptomic data are internally consistent across >10 independent studies (RNA-seq and microarray), all showing peak expression at late schizont/merozoite stages with dramatic downregulation during rings and trophozoites. The coexpression module is exceptionally coherent, dominated by known invasion proteins and apical organelle components, with the top GO term being "entry into host." The strongly negative piggyBac fitness score independently confirms essentiality. The main limitation is the absence of localisation and protein interaction data, which prevents definitive assignment to a specific organelle (rhoptry vs. microneme vs. surface). The low proteomic detection (3/63 samples) is consistent with stage-specific expression rather than contradicting the prediction.

## Experimental Validation

To confirm this function: (1) Generate an epitope-tagged (e.g., HA or GFP) parasite line and perform immunofluorescence microscopy at schizont stage to determine subcellular localisation (rhoptry, microneme, or surface). (2) Perform conditional knockdown (e.g., glmS ribozyme or TetR-DOZI system) timed to schizogony and measure invasion efficiency by flow cytometry. (3) Conduct co-immunoprecipitation followed by mass spectrometry at the schizont stage to identify protein interaction partners and confirm association with known invasion complexes. (4) Bioinformatically, perform signal peptide and transmembrane domain prediction (SignalP, TMHMM), search for homologs in other *Plasmodium* species to identify conserved invasion-related domains, and check for structural similarity to known invasion ligands using AlphaFold2.
